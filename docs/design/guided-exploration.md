# Guided exploration UX for the fractal explorer

Status: implemented; this file is the design as approved on 2026-10-09 and the code is the truth where they differ.

## Problem

The page is a control panel with a picture beside it. About five stacked cards (Presets, Navigation, View Info, Rendering Parameters) hold duplicate controls: position is a pair of sliders, a readout and two number boxes; presets are a dropdown and a row of buttons; zoom is a slider, buttons and the wheel. Nothing connects one interesting view to the next, there is no way to retrace a path, and the one guided feature (Random walk) is a checkbox. Preset button labels are also clipped (fr-wtk).

## Goals

- A casual visitor is delighted within seconds with almost no controls (owner pick: casual wanderer first).
- The picture is the interface: it fills the page and is driven by drag, wheel, pinch and keys.
- "What is interesting near here" is always visible, and clicking it takes you there.
- You can retrace your steps (Back, Forward) and the browser's own Back works.
- Every existing capability stays reachable. Nothing is removed, only moved.

## Non-goals

Accounts, shared galleries, server storage, new cloud services, new fractal types, a guided tour with captions, a gallery-first home page.

## Design

### Screen

- The canvas fills the viewport in a 4:3 frame that keeps its aspect ratio.
- A slim top bar floats over it: Back and Forward (left), Surprise me (centre), Settings and Help (right). It fades after about two seconds without input and returns on pointer movement or touch.
- A bottom strip, "Nearby, worth a look", shows 5 or 6 thumbnails (about 64 px wide) in the current colour scheme, plus a "more" tile.
- Settings is one drawer (a bottom sheet on narrow screens) holding what exists today: fractal, Julia constant with Custom, colour scheme, iterations and Auto iterations, centre and bounds coordinates, Download PNG (and export sizes if fr-gqe lands), saved views, Copy link, Auto-wander. The position sliders and the View Info card are dropped; their information lives in the coordinate boxes.

### Interactions

- Drag, wheel, pinch, arrow keys, `+` and `-` behave as today.
- Clicking a thumbnail flies the canvas to that place. First version is a crossfade between the old and new picture; a rendered smooth zoom is a later bead.
- Every move (a click, a Surprise, a preset, a manual jump) is a history entry. Back and Forward step through them. Browser Back works because the view is already in the URL hash (view links, fr-14o).
- Surprise me flies to a random suggestion. It replaces the Random walk checkbox; Auto-wander (the old walk) moves into Settings.
- Narrow screens: the strip is a horizontally scrolling row; Settings is a bottom sheet.

### Places

A place is `{ id, name, fractal, juliaConstant?, centre, zoom, scheme? }`. It is a built-in preset by another name, so presets, saved views and the link codec are reused rather than replaced.

### Suggestions

`suggest(view, catalogue, n)` is a pure function. It ranks places by closeness (centre distance scaled by zoom), prefers slightly deeper zooms so each click is a step in, never returns the current place, and returns the top n. Surprise me picks one at random from the result. Step 1 is a curated catalogue: roughly 15 to 25 places for the Mandelbrot set and 8 to 12 each for Julia and Burning Ship, chosen from live contact sheets and measured, as the current presets were. Step 2 (later) adds computed suggestions behind the same function: probe a grid of candidate views at tiny resolution, score by edge or detail density, keep a few that differ from each other and from the curated ones. The UI does not change when step 2 lands.

### Thumbnails

Each tile renders on demand into a small off-screen canvas (64x48) with the existing renderer at low iterations, cached by place and colour scheme. They start only after the main picture settles and cancel on navigation like any render. A failed or cancelled thumbnail shows a flat colour placeholder and stays clickable.

### Failure handling

- Storage or history disabled: the strip works and Back and Forward use in-memory history.
- Garbage link: default view, as today.
- A place that fails validation is rejected by the catalogue guard test, never at run time.

## Testing

- Node-only vitest for the pure parts: `suggest` (ordering, ties, empty catalogue, a view far from every place), history (push, back, forward, forward list truncated by a new move), place validation (each place resolves with `resolveFractalKind` and is within the precision limit).
- A catalogue guard test renders each place at low resolution and fails if the picture is nearly blank.
- UI behaviour by source-walk tests in the existing style; each landing is checked live on staging in the browser.

4. Nearby strip: thumbnails, caching, click-to-fly with crossfade. Depends on 1, 2, 3.
5. Surprise me replaces the Random walk checkbox; mobile bottom sheet polish. Depends on 1, 3.

## Release

No feature flag. Beads 1 and 2 are invisible; 3 changes the layout without losing a control; 4 and 5 add the strip and Surprise. The release tag stays the owner's call.

## As built

Where the build differs from the text above. The code is the truth.

- **Top bar order.** The bar holds the title on the left, Surprise me and Tour in the centre, and Back, Forward, Math, Help and Settings together on the right (`src/components/ui/TopBar.tsx`; Math arrived with the Math card below). The design put Back and Forward on the left. Under `sm` (640 px) the title is drawn for a screen reader only, to leave the width to the seven buttons.
- **Pinch.** Built after the first landing (fr-5p9). Two fingers on the canvas zoom by the ratio of their distance to the last step's, about the point between them, and moving both together pans (`src/lib/pinch.ts`, `ZoomPanManager` in `src/lib/zoomPan.ts`). The zoom is held to 1x to 10000x before the anchor is worked out, so a pinch against a limit keeps its point under the fingers. `canvasNavigation.ts` cancels `touchmove` while two fingers are down, so the page neither zooms nor scrolls. A touch is one gesture from the first finger down to the last up: lifting one finger of a pinch carries on as a one-finger pan from where that finger is, and the full-resolution render is deferred until the last finger lifts. A pinch only rewrites the current history entry, as the wheel does. The wheel, `+` and `-`, and the Zoom In, Zoom Out and Zoom Level controls in Settings zoom as before.
- **Place fields.** A place is `{ id, name, fractal, variant?, centerX, centerY, zoom, colorScheme? }` (`src/lib/places.ts`): the centre is two numbers and the scheme is `colorScheme`. The catalogue is 19 Mandelbrot, 10 Julia, 10 Burning Ship, 9 Tricorn, 12 Multibrot, 15 Newton, 6 Celtic, 5 Perpendicular Ship, 6 Magnet and 6 Phoenix places; `variant` is the Julia constant, the Multibrot power ("3", "4" or "5"), the Newton polynomial ("3", "4", "5" or "cycle"), the Magnet type ("1" or "2") or the Phoenix constant ("Classic", "Spirals" or "Hourglass"), and `suggest` lists the places of the view's own variant first for any of them.
- **The strip does not fade.** The design had only the bar fading, and the first build faded the strip with it, which hid the page's main way of moving from a visitor lost in an empty field. Only the bar fades now (fr-s8f); the strip is always shown. `NearbyStrip` takes an optional `hidden` prop that renders nothing and requests no thumbnails, which Tour mode uses (see **Tour** below).
- **Strip size.** The strip shows six tiles at a time from a pool of 18, and the More tile rotates to the next six, wrapping (`src/lib/nearbyStrip.ts`). The design said 5 or 6. The suggestions are worked out again only after a deliberate move (a preset, typed coordinates, a fractal or Julia constant pick, a tile, Surprise me, a link, Back or Forward), not while dragging, wheeling or keying.
- **Thumbnail timing.** Thumbnails do not wait for the picture to settle by starting late. They are asked for as the tiles mount and the store is paused while a full render is in flight (`paused={isRendering}`), so a picture already running is never slowed. The one render in flight when the pause starts is allowed to finish. A thumbnail draws at the main picture's Auto iteration count, not half of it as first built, because a pixel's colour is its iteration count over the maximum and half the count shifts the whole palette.
- **Thumbnail colours.** The design said thumbnails are drawn in the current colour scheme. A click applies the place's own scheme, so a tile drawn in the page's colours did not match its destination. A tile is drawn in `placeColorScheme(place, current)` (`src/lib/places.ts`), the same call the click makes: the place's scheme when it names one, else the page's. The cache is keyed by place and that scheme, so changing the page's scheme repaints only the tiles whose place names none (today every catalogue place names one). A test renders each place's thumbnail beside the picture its click draws at 64x48 and holds them to a mean difference of 1 per channel (`placeThumbnails.test.ts`).
- **Surprise me.** It picks at random from the first six suggestions (`SURPRISE_CANDIDATES` in `src/lib/surprise.ts`), not from a longer list, and goes through the Nearby tile's own click handler, so it has the tile's crossfade and its single history entry. The button is disabled when no place is near enough to offer.
- **History.** `src/lib/journey.ts` is the page's record of its entries; the browser holds the entries themselves, and Back and Forward call `history.back()` and `history.forward()`. If `pushState` throws (a sandboxed frame, a browser cap) the page writes the fragment with `replaceState` instead and keeps the step in its record. The design's in-memory history for a browser with history disabled was not built; Back and Forward always go through the browser's.
- **Moves that make an entry.** A preset, Apply Coordinates, a pick from the Fractal select, a pick from the fractal's variant select (a Julia constant or a Multibrot power), a fractal button or named variant chip in the switcher, a Nearby tile or Surprise me, a Reverse or Shift change (one entry per gesture, marked on the slider's pointer-down or first moving key), switching Cycle colours off (which stops the palette where it is), Reset colours, and a pick on the Julia map (one entry per drag, marked at pointer-down). Typing in the Real and Imaginary boxes of a custom constant does not, and neither does a drag, wheel, key, slider or Auto-wander step; those rewrite the current entry.
- **Settings contents.** The drawer holds, in order, the cards Presets, Navigation (Zoom Level, Zoom Out, Zoom In, Reset View, Copy link and Copy tour link), Auto-wander (the checkbox, a line saying the same switch is on the picture, and the Seconds between steps box), Coordinates (a read-out line over the Real (X) and Imaginary (Y) boxes), Rendering Parameters (Fractal, the fractal's variant select — Julia constant with Custom, Multibrot Power, Newton Polynomial or Magnet Type — with the Julia map and the Real and Imaginary boxes under it, Auto iterations, Maximum Iterations, Color Scheme, then Reverse, Shift and Reset colours, then the Cycle colours switch and speed), Canvas Resolution (Width and Height boxes and three size buttons), Manual Coordinates, saved views (Custom Presets) and Download PNG (Shape and Export size above the button). Copy link and Copy tour link sit in the Navigation card.
- **Auto-wander** is its own card, headed "Auto-wander", directly after Navigation, rather than a line among the others.
- **Presets.** The Presets card is the dropdown alone, listing the built-in and saved views for the fractal on the canvas (six Mandelbrot, five Julia, two Burning Ship, two Perpendicular Ship, two Tricorn, three Multibrot, two Newton, two Celtic, two Magnet and two Phoenix built-ins). The row of preset buttons, and the clipped labels of fr-wtk with it, are gone.
- **Failure handling.** The catalogue guard test (`src/lib/__tests__/places.test.ts`) holds every place to the link round trip and a 64x48 render of at least 5% non-corner pixels, as designed. A thumbnail that fails to render caches a flat grey placeholder for the life of the store and its tile stays clickable.
- **Tour.** A screensaver tour (owner-approved 2026-10-10, fr-w3p) that the design's non-goals ("a guided tour with captions") left room for: no captions, no sound. It starts after about 20 s with no input while the window has focus (15 s once a tour has played and been stopped, `TOUR_RESUME_MS`), when the address is `#tour` (or a view link carries `tour=1`), or when the top bar's Tour button is pressed, and it never starts over the Settings drawer, Help, a focused text field, a background tab or a render in flight. While it plays the page hides the top bar, the strip, the progress line and the pointer, and hops through the catalogue every 8 s with a 1.5 s crossfade (a cut under reduced motion; the dwell is the same), choosing with `nextTourStop` (`src/lib/tour.ts`). Any key, touch, click, pointer move or wheel stops it at once and the controls come back on the place it was showing. The clock is `src/lib/tourMode.ts`. The address bar is not written while a tour plays, so the history entry the tour began in keeps the view before it and a long tour adds nothing; stopping adds one entry for the place on screen, so Back returns to the view before the tour (the first draft of the bead had each hop replace the entry, which would have lost that view). A best-effort screen wake lock is held while it plays (`src/lib/wakeLock.ts`). Auto-wander is held for the same time. The Tour button sits beside Surprise me, so the bar's order is the title, then Surprise me and Tour, then Back, Forward, Math, Help and Settings.
- **Auto-wander hops; a stopped Tour resumes sooner.** Owner-approved 2026-10-10 (fr-75l), because Auto-wander's small random steps stayed on one fractal and drifted into dull areas. Every `WANDER_HOP_EVERY`th step (4, in `src/lib/wanderController.ts`) is a hop: the controller calls the step handler with `{ hop: true }` and the page asks `nextTourStop` for the next place, from a state of its own (not the tour's) and the walk's seeded generator, so the Tour's rules apply (never the place on screen, a recent window, about one hop in six changes fractal). A hop goes through `handlePlaceSelect(place, { replace: true })`, the tile's handler with the tile's 400 ms crossfade, the place's scheme and Julia constant, and the current history entry rewritten instead of a new one (a drift does the same; the tour's `tour` option implies `replace`). The other three steps are `nextWanderView` as before; `wander.ts` is unchanged. A drift whose picture has settled blank (`canvasIsBlank`, the fr-2oa detector, handed to the controller with `setBlankDetector` and asked from the page's effect on the settled render, `notifySettled`) is replaced at once by a hop, which also restarts the count of drifts; a view moved by hand first is not judged. In `src/lib/tourMode.ts`, `TOUR_RESUME_MS` (15 s) replaces `TOUR_IDLE_START_MS` (20 s) as the idle wait once a tour has played in the page session (`hasPlayed`), whether the phase is `stopped` or an ask has since made it `armed`; a cold page still waits 20 s, and every condition that holds a start back is as it was.
- **Fractal switcher.** Added after the design (owner-approved 2026-10-10, fr-rab) because changing fractal took Settings, Rendering Parameters and a dropdown. `src/components/ui/FractalSwitcher.tsx` draws Mandelbrot, Julia, Burning Ship, Perp. Ship, Tricorn, Multibrot, Newton, Celtic, Magnet and Phoenix as buttons over the top left of the canvas, the current one `aria-pressed`, and a row of chips for Julia's four named constants and Custom while Julia is current (and, since fr-abq, a Power row of z^3, z^4 and z^5 while the Multibrot is; since fr-ztk a Polynomial row of z^3 - 1, z^4 - 1, z^5 - 1 and z^3 - 2z + 2 while the Newton fractal is; and since fr-ihs a Type row of Type I and Type II while the Magnet is; and since fr-04l a Phoenix constant row of Classic, Spirals and Hourglass while Phoenix is). It never fades and is hidden during a tour and while Settings or Help is open. A fractal button is a jump to that fractal's featured place (`FEATURED_PLACE_IDS` and `featuredPlace` in `src/lib/places.ts`: Seahorse Valley, the Classic spirals, the armada, the whole Perpendicular Ship, the whole three-armed Tricorn, a small copy of the z^3 set, Newton's junction, the whole Celtic set, and the Magnet's drift of disks, which is not a whole-set view because the settled set the picture's black stands for is nearly the whole plane) through the Nearby tile's handler, so it has the crossfade, the place's own colours and one history entry. A named chip goes to the whole set of that constant, power or magnetic type (`wholeSetPlaceFor`); Custom picks Custom as the Settings select does and opens Settings with focus in the Real box. The Settings selects stay and show the same state. The blank-picture hint moved down a row (two with the chips) to clear it. The label "Perp. Ship" is the row's one shortened name, so nine buttons keep the row readable on a phone; the Math card, a link's line and the Help overlay give the fractal its full name.
- **Tricorn.** A fourth fractal, added after the design (owner-approved 2026-10-10, fr-vpv): the Mandelbrot loop with z conjugated first, z -> conj(z)^2 + c (`src/lib/tricorn.ts`). It joins the switcher as a fourth button, the Fractal select, two built-in views, nine catalogue places (`TRICORN_PLACES`) and `FEATURED_PLACE_IDS` (the whole Tricorn, fr-hbe: a view zoomed in on the real axis is the Mandelbrot set, but the whole set is not), with no special case in the Nearby strip, Surprise me, Tour or Auto-wander. Its opening view is the origin at zoom 1, and it takes no variant, so the switcher draws no second row for it.
- **Multibrot.** A fifth fractal, added after the design (owner-approved 2026-10-10, fr-abq): the Mandelbrot loop with a higher power, z -> z^d + c for d = 3, 4, 5 (`src/lib/multibrot.ts`: `createMultibrotKind`, and `parseMultibrotPower`, which reads only the plain spellings "3", "4" and "5"). It is the first fractal after Julia to use the variant mechanism: `fractalVariants("multibrot")` is a row captioned "Power" with chips z^3, z^4, z^5 and no Custom, so the switcher and the Settings select draw it with no new control; a link carries the power as `k=3|4|5`, and a saved view stores it as `variant` (Julia keeps `juliaConstant`; both are read). The page keeps one variant across fractals, so going to a fractal that takes one brings a value it can draw (`variantOn`). Its opening view is the origin at zoom 1, which holds all three whole sets; the switcher's fifth button lands on a small copy of the z^3 set (`FEATURED_PLACE_IDS`), the chips on the whole set of their power, there are three built-in views and twelve catalogue places, four per power, and Surprise me, Tour and Auto-wander pick them up with no further code.
- **Celtic Mandelbrot.** A seventh fractal, added after the design (owner-approved 2026-10-11, fr-gx5): the Mandelbrot loop with the real part of the square folded by an absolute value, z -> (|Re(z^2)| + i Im(z^2)) + c (`src/lib/celtic.ts`, `celticKind`), on the same escape-time convention as the Tricorn and the Burning Ship so the colour schemes, recolour, Shift/Reverse and cycling work unchanged. It joins the switcher as a seventh button, the Fractal select, two built-in views, six catalogue places (`CELTIC_PLACES`) and `FEATURED_PLACE_IDS` (the whole set, which is also its opening view, at (-0.8, 0) and zoom 1), takes no variant (so no second row), and has a Math card descriptor, a probe and an orbit plot like the others. Its real axis is the Mandelbrot set's (the fold has nothing negative to flip while the imaginary part is 0), so it is listed in `REAL_AXIS_MATCHES_MANDELBROT` and no place is zoomed in on the axis. The pre-built previews gained six `public/og/celtic-*.jpg` pictures.
- **Magnet.** A ninth fractal, added after the design (owner-approved 2026-10-11, fr-ihs): the two rational maps of the magnetic model, `z -> ((z^2 + c - 1)/(2z + c - 2))^2` for Type I and `z -> ((z^3 + 3(c-1)z + (c-1)(c-2)) / (3z^2 + 3(c-2)z + (c-1)(c-2) + 1))^2` for Type II (`src/lib/magnet.ts`, `createMagnetKind`), both from `z0 = 0`, on the shared smooth-colouring convention so the colour schemes, recolour, Shift/Reverse and cycling work unchanged. z = 1 is a superattracting fixed point of either map for every c, and the set the picture is named for is the c whose orbit settles there: that is nearly the whole plane, so the picture's flat black is most of a frame and the colours draw the island of c that runs off to infinity instead, with the fractal boundary between the two basins through it. A point is outside when |z| passes 100 (their orbits wander out to about 4 first) and the smoothing carries that radius: `n + 1 - log2(log|z| / log 100)`. A vanishing denominator is a pole of the map, counted as the orbit's last step rather than divided by zero. It is the second fractal to take a variant after the Multibrot: a Type row offers Type I and Type II (`k=1` or `k=2` in a link, stored as `variant`), each with its own whole-set place, and the fractal opens on 1.3 at zoom 1, the widest view there is, which holds both islands across with the settled black either side. It joins the switcher as a ninth button, the Fractal select, two built-in views (both magnified: the settled set is nearly the whole plane, so no overview view passes the per-fractal guard), six catalogue places (`MAGNET_PLACES`) and `FEATURED_PLACE_IDS`, which here is the drift of Type I disks rather than a whole-set view for the same reason. The pre-built previews gained six `public/og/magnet-*.jpg` pictures. Its orbit plot draws no escape circle, since its radius is 100 rather than 2.
- **Phoenix.** A tenth fractal, added after the design (owner-approved 2026-10-12, fr-04l): a Julia set whose step also uses the point before the last, `z(n+1) = z(n)^2 + c + p z(n-1)` with `z(-1) = 0` and the pixel as `z(0)` (`src/lib/phoenix.ts`, `createPhoenixKind`, on the shared smooth-colouring convention, escape at `|z|^2 > 4`, so the colour schemes, recolour, Shift/Reverse and cycling work unchanged). A kind keeps its own state, so the orbit loop carries `z(n-1)` and the renderer is untouched; `orbit` and `escapeInfo` walk the same two-step rule, so the Math card's plot follows it. The constant is a pair, `c` (the real part of the carried point) and `p` (its imaginary part), and is the fractal's variant, as Julia's is: a Phoenix constant row of Classic (`c = 0.5667`, `p = -0.5`), Spirals (`0.3`, `0.5`) and Hourglass (`0.5667`, `-0.55`), named in a link as `k=Classic|Spirals|Hourglass` and stored as `variant`, with no Custom choice. "Classic" is also a Julia constant's name; each fractal reads a `k` only if it can draw it, so the two do not collide. With `p = 0` it is exactly the Julia set of `c`, and with `c` and `p` real the set is mirrored about the real axis. It opens on the origin at zoom 1, which holds all three sets. It joins the switcher as a tenth button, the Fractal select, two built-in views, six catalogue places (`PHOENIX_PLACES`: the whole of each constant, feathers and curls on Classic's flame, a spiral arm of Spirals) and `FEATURED_PLACE_IDS` (the whole Classic set, which is also its opening view), and has a Math card descriptor with a one-sentence account of the memory term, a probe and an orbit plot like the others. The pre-built previews gained six `public/og/phoenix-*.jpg` pictures.
- **Perpendicular Ship.** An eighth fractal, added after the design (owner-approved 2026-10-11, fr-d1s): the Burning Ship's cousin with only one half of the step folded, z -> (|Re z| - i Im z)^2 + c, that is x' = x^2 - y^2 + cx and y' = -2|x|y + cy (`src/lib/perpendicularShip.ts`, `perpendicularShipKind`), on the same escape-time convention as the Tricorn and the Celtic set so the colour schemes, recolour, Shift/Reverse and cycling work unchanged. It joins the switcher as an eighth button (labelled "Perp. Ship", the one short name, so the row stays readable on a phone), the Fractal select, two built-in views, five catalogue places (`PERPENDICULAR_SHIP_PLACES`) and `FEATURED_PLACE_IDS` (the whole ship, which is also its opening view, at (-0.55, 0) and zoom 1), takes no variant (so no second row), and has a Math card descriptor, a probe and an orbit plot like the others. It is mirrored about the real axis, so only the upper half's places are named; its real axis is the Mandelbrot set's (the fold has nothing to do while the imaginary part is 0), so it is listed in `REAL_AXIS_MATCHES_MANDELBROT` and no place is zoomed in on the axis. The pre-built previews gained five `public/og/perpendicular-ship-*.jpg` pictures.
- **Newton.** A sixth fractal, added after the design (owner-approved 2026-10-10, fr-y6u): Newton's method for z^3 - 1 (`src/lib/newton.ts`, `newtonKind`), the first fractal that is not an escape-time set. Each pixel is coloured by which of the three cube roots of 1 its orbit reaches, shaded by how fast. The renderer and the colour mapping take one number per point, so the kind packs the root and the speed into it: each root owns a third of 0..maxIterations (`NEWTON_BAND`) and the step count puts the point inside it by s/(s + 6), 4% in from each edge, so every scheme shows three colour families and the packed number never reaches the cap, which is black (z = 0, a non-finite orbit and a point the cap cannot settle). It joins the switcher as a sixth button, the Fractal select, two built-in views, nine catalogue places (`NEWTON_PLACES`) and `FEATURED_PLACE_IDS` (the pinwheel where the three basins meet); Surprise me, Tour and Auto-wander pick it up with no further code. Its opening view is the origin at zoom 1, which holds all three roots. The row of six buttons is about 375 px wide, so it scrolls sideways on a 375 px screen (and on 320 px), with the lit button scrolled into view. The blank-picture hint stays sound: the three-basin view shows three colours and is not blank in any scheme, while a deep view inside one basin is.
- **Newton's polynomials.** A variant row, added after the design (owner-approved 2026-10-11, fr-ztk): the fractal is a family of four polynomials rather than one picture, so `fractalVariants("newton")` is a row captioned "Polynomial" of z^3 - 1 (the picture the app has always drawn, and what a link with no `k` names), z^4 - 1, z^5 - 1 and z^3 - 2z + 2, with no Custom, and a link carries the choice as `k=3|4|5|cycle` or nothing. `src/lib/newton.ts` now describes a polynomial as its roots and the arithmetic of one step (`NewtonPolynomial`; the step is `z - p(z)/p'(z)`, written out per polynomial), and `createNewtonKind(polynomial)` builds the kind for one, memoized so a resolve is stable and the z^3 - 1 kind is the `newtonKind` export the rest of the app already uses; `parseNewtonPolynomial` reads only the app's own four spellings and `newtonPolynomialFor` falls back to z^3 - 1, as the Multibrot's parser falls back to 3. Each root owns 1/n of the colour range, so a polynomial of n roots shows n colour families and nothing else in the packing, the smoothing or the renderer changes. z^3 - 2z + 2 is the one whose map has an attracting two-step loop beside the origin (0 -> 1 -> 0), so the orbits there never reach a root: they run the cap and are painted black, which is the dark disc the picture is built around, and `escapeInfo` reports them as unsettled like any other cap-bound orbit. The switcher's chip row and the Settings select draw the four from the descriptor with no new control; each has its own whole-set place (`wholeSetPlaceFor`, all four at the origin at zoom 1, the one frame that holds every root), two places per polynomial were added to the catalogue (15 Newton places) with their pre-built previews, the Math card's rule, setting and sentences follow the polynomial, and two built-in views still open the fractal on z^3 - 1.
- **Auto-wander pill.** Owner-approved 2026-10-10 (fr-q9t), because the only switch for Auto-wander was a checkbox inside Settings and a first-time visitor had no way to find it or to turn it back on. `WanderControl` (`src/components/ui/WanderControl.tsx`) is a compact pill drawn after the Nearby strip in the canvas's wrapper, bottom right, an `aria-pressed` button whose accessible name is "Auto-wander" in both states: off it shows a play glyph and "Auto-wander", on a pause glyph and "Wandering", with " · N s" while the controller's countdown runs and no render is in flight. It calls `handleWanderChange`, the Settings checkbox's own handler, so the two are one state; Settings keeps the checkbox and the seconds box, and says the same switch is on the picture. It never fades and is hidden for a tour and while Settings or Help is open, as the switcher is. - **Image route.** _Superseded by the third entry below (fr-5om): the route no longer draws anything, and the renderer, its limits and the PNG encoder are gone. What follows is how it was._ Added after the design (owner-approved 2026-10-10, fr-c4z), the first half of per-view link previews (the second, Copy link and the page's og:image pointing at it, is fr-cr5; see the next entry), so nothing a visitor sees changed. `GET /api/og?<view link>` (`src/app/api/og/route.ts`) drew the view the query names, in the link codec's own format, as a 1200x630 PNG on the server with the app's own maths. The pixel pipeline moved out of `FractalRenderer` into `src/lib/renderRgba.ts` (`renderRgba`, no canvas), which the canvas renderer now draws every row through, so the browser and the route cannot disagree (a test holds them byte for byte for every fractal and variant); `src/lib/pngEncode.ts` is a minimal PNG encoder on `node:zlib`. The route is strict where the page is forgiving: a query that is not a version-1 view, or has a known key whose value is refused, is a 400 before any work. The 1.91:1 card keeps the page's 4:3 view's height and centre and widens to the card's shape, so a visitor's whole view is inside it; iterations are the page's rule held to 400. It is bounded as an unauthenticated CPU endpoint on a scale-to-zero service: an LRU of 40 PNGs, one render at a time per instance with a queue of 2 (a further request is a 503 with `Retry-After: 2`), an 8 s budget per render, and `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable` on a 200; the limits are injectable (`src/lib/ogLimits.ts`, `ogRoute.ts`). One render costs about 350 to 460 ms on a development Mac for a deep Mandelbrot or Julia view. Details and measurements are in `CLAUDE.md`'s Version Note for this route.
- **Per-view previews.** The second half (owner-approved 2026-10-10, fr-cr5). A crawler never sees the part of an address after `#`, so the view travels in the query for sharing: Copy link copies `<origin><path>?<fragment>` (`copyLinkUrl`, `src/lib/viewQuery.ts`) while the address bar keeps the hash form and every other write is as it was. A page loaded with a query reads it once at mount (`readLocationLink`: a hash wins if both are present; otherwise a query that is a view link, one the codec reads at least one field of or that asks for `tour=1`, is taken as the fragment and goes through `decodeView` and `viewLinkToUpdates` like a hash), then `history.replaceState` rewrites the address to `<path>#<fragment>` and drops the query, so the follower, Back and Forward and the journey see the form they always saw. A query that is not a view link is left alone. The explorer moved to `src/app/FractalExplorer.tsx` and `src/app/page.tsx` became a server page, because only a page is given `searchParams` and a layout is not: its `generateMetadata` calls `pageMetadata(origin, searchParams)` (`src/lib/pageMetadata.ts`, which the layout uses with no parameters for the static head). A request previews as its view only when `parseOgQuery` accepts it, the same strictness `/api/og` uses, so a query the image route would refuse is the static card; the URL carries the view re-encoded from the decoded state and the extra title line is built from the decoded state's labels (`describeViewLine`: "Julia set, Dendrite, 400x"), never from the request's text. No new route, resource or credential.
- **Pre-built previews.** The per-view card stopped being drawn on demand (owner-approved 2026-10-10, fr-xnl). `/api/og` is an unauthenticated CPU endpoint, so any stranger could ask it for renders; instead the picture a preview names is built ahead of time and served as a static file. `scripts/make-og-previews.mjs` (run by hand as `npm run make-og-previews`, never by the gate, the image or CI) draws one 1200x630 JPEG for every catalogue place into `public/og/<place-id>.jpg`, through the app's own pixel pipeline — `renderRgba` at `cardBounds`, the place's scheme, Auto's iteration count and smooth counts, which is the picture the page draws on landing there — and refuses a picture that is nearly one colour or too heavy; the committed set is held to the catalogue by a test. A shared view previews as **the nearest place of its own fractal** (`nearestPlace` in `src/lib/places.ts`, `ogImageFor` in `src/lib/ogImage.ts`): the place the view is on when it is within 1% of one in centre and zoom, else the nearest centre on the screen the link names, with a place of the same Julia constant or Multibrot power preferred, so a card always shows the same kind of fractal the link does and — unlike `/api/og` — the same picture every time, which is also what makes it cacheable. og:image and twitter:image become `<origin>/og/<place-id>.jpg` with `image/jpeg`, the alt naming the place, and og:url, the title and the descriptions as they were; `/og/*` carries `Cache-Control: public, max-age=86400, stale-while-revalidate=604800` from a `headers()` rule in `next.config.js`, and every other path keeps the headers it had. Nothing in the head points at `/api/og` any more; the route still drew for a request of its own until fr-5om (the entry below), which turned it into a redirect and deleted its renderer, its limits and the encoder with them. The landing asked for at most 200 KB a file at quality 80: the encoder is `sharp` (the one `make-og-card.mjs` already uses, installed with Next.js), which draws a picture at 80 and steps down only as far as it must to fit — 67 of the 69 places at 80, the two deepest filigree frames at 70 and 67 — for a set of about 4.6 MB, and the measurements are in the script's header.
- **The image route stops rendering.** fr-5om (owner concern, 2026-10-10, about cost abuse), the follow-on the entry above named. `GET /api/og?<view link>` no longer draws: it answers **308** to the pre-built picture the view previews as, or to the static card for a query that is not a view, so a preview URL a crawler or chat app cached before the pictures existed still shows a picture and no request can make the server spend CPU on a render. The picture is chosen by `previewForQuery` (`src/lib/viewPreview.ts`), the same function the page's own head uses, fed the request's search string — one rule, so the URL a crawler is sent to is the one the shared page names; the origin is read from the request through `siteOrigin`, validated, never the raw `Host`. The renderer is gone with the code that bounded it: `src/lib/ogRoute.ts`, `src/lib/ogLimits.ts` (its LRU, render gate and 8 s budget) and `src/lib/pngEncode.ts`, with their tests; `src/lib/ogView.ts` keeps only the strict query reader and the card frame the preview generator draws in (`cardBounds`), and `viewPreview.ts` gained `previewForQuery`, with `previewFor` now a thin wrapper over it. `renderRgba` stays, because the canvas and the preview generator use it. A source-walk test holds the route to importing no renderer, no encoder and no cache, and a handler test drives valid, invalid, empty and hostile queries to their 308s, never a drawn body.
- **Math probe.** Added after the design (owner-approved 2026-10-10, fr-ycw; layer 2 of 3 of the math design, the orbit plot following it in fr-l62 — see below). With the Math card expanded, pointing at the picture adds a `Point` row — the complex number under the pointer, six significant digits, and a swatch of the colour the picture paints that pixel — and an `Orbit` row saying what the point's orbit did: "Escaped at step N", "Inside the set" for an escape-time set, "Settles on root k after N steps" for Newton, or "Never settled in N steps" for a Newton point the cap cannot settle. The point is read with `pixelToComplex` and the bounds on screen, the count from the kind's own `escapeInfo(point, maxIterations)` (an optional method on `FractalKind`, implemented by every kind this build ships, and read off the count for a kind that lacks it), and the colour from the colour mapping at the count a full render paints, clamped as a `Uint8ClampedArray` clamps — so the swatch is the pixel on screen, held there by a parity test over a drawn view (`src/lib/mathProbe.ts`). It runs only while the card is expanded and on screen: collapsed or hidden, no listener is attached and nothing is computed; live, the pointer's handlers only remember where it is and the work is one point per animation frame at most, read in the frame's callback rather than in the handler. The view and the kind are the effect's dependencies, so a picture that moves under a resting pointer re-reads the same place, and the pointer leaving the picture clears it (a finger lifting does not: a tap's point stays up to be read). The renderer and every kind's count are unchanged.
- **Orbit plot.** Added after the design (owner-approved 2026-10-10, fr-l62; layer 3 of 3 of the math design, after the Math card and the probe). Expanded, the Math card draws the path the point under the pointer takes: a 220 px square SVG under the probe's two rows, with the axes and their unit ticks, the radius of 2 drawn faintly for every fractal that escapes, the orbit as dots joined by a line and coloured through the picture's own palette, the start ringed and the escaping point solid white, and the last three points written out as a + bi under it. The points come from `FractalKind.orbit(point, maxSteps)`, an optional method on the kind interface like `escapeInfo`, implemented by all six kinds and answered with the point alone by one that cannot walk an orbit (`orbitFor`, `src/lib/mathProbe.ts`); the walk is capped at 60 steps (`ORBIT_MAX_STEPS`) whatever the iteration cap is, so a point inside the set spirals for sixty steps and a point outside it ends on the last point that left the radius. `orbitToPlot(orbit, size, minExtent)` is the pure scaling: the frame is the orbit's own reach, never smaller than the escape circle and never more than twice it, a point past the frame is pinned to the edge rather than dropped, and a value that is not a number at all is read as the origin, so no orbit can put a NaN on the page. The plot is a `role="img"` labelled by `describeOrbit` ("Orbit of the point, 7 steps, escaped", "first 60 steps, stays inside" when the walk stopped short, Newton's own wording for its roots). A Play control walks it a step every 120 ms, off until asked for and disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`; a pointer move re-reads the point and the animation is tied to the orbit it started on, so it stops without a listener of its own. The probe's own lifecycle is unchanged: nothing is computed while the card is collapsed or hidden.
